Dialogue Excerpt: Explaining the Mean to a Stubborn Classmate
Classmate: "Okay, I see the median is 112.50 PKR for Staple A, but why isn't the mean the 'typical' price? Mean is just the mathematical average of everything!"

Student: "Think of it like a room of ten people where nine make 50,000 PKR and one person makes 10,000,000 PKR. If you take the mean, it looks like everyone is a millionaire, even though 90% of the room is nowhere near that. In our data, that 1,250 PKR spike in Staple A is that millionaire—it drags the average way up even though every weekly price was around 110 PKR."

Classmate: "Ah, so the mean gets bullied by extremes, but the median just sits in the middle and ignores them?"

Student: "Exactly. That's why the median represents the typical experience, while the mean represents the total sum spread evenly."

Classmate: "Okay, I finally get it. The mean isn't wrong mathematically, but it's terrible at showing what a normal person actually pays when there's a typo in the ledger!"

Overreach in the First Draft
In my first draft, I asserted that "consumers felt the pinch of rising market inflation and changed their purchasing habits." This went far beyond what the data allowed because the teaching ledger contains zero information about consumer behavior, sales volume, or macroeconomic inflation—it only records static, illustrative weekly price points in two model cities.
